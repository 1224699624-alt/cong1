\section{Conclusion}

Instance-derived seam supervision uses information already present in indexed annotations to expose a failure that region overlap largely ignores. On a matched mature nnU-Net comparison, it reduces image-level component under-counting and gap-region false positives with nearly unchanged Dice. The improvement is meaningful but incomplete: recall and surface-distance results show that unweighted seam classification can trade false bridges for local erosion or outliers. The next step is therefore not a stronger claim, but a stricter mechanism---a decoupled confidence-weighted separation loss with foreground support, followed by cross-backbone and demographic subgroup validation.
